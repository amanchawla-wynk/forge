# Public Guidance Disciplines Proxy PRD Review

**Public product-development guidance can give Forge a defensible proxy panel for discovering omissions, contradictions, and unsafe ambiguity in the MicroDrama recommendations PRD, but it cannot validate Forge for any organization. AI agents are not independent human reviewers: repeated agents, prompts, or source perspectives do not establish inter-rater reliability, organization-specific expectations, calibrated severity, or predictive validity.** The three supplied proxy reviews converge on a document-level judgment that the PDF is not ready to serve as the sole controlling specification for implementation or broad launch, chiefly because its skip taxonomy, launch source, state persistence, ranking authority, and safety dependencies do not resolve to one behavior, while experiment, privacy, accessibility, reliability, and rollback evidence is incomplete or absent. That consensus should produce a source-bound diagnostic artifact, explicitly typed as synthetic public-guidance proxy evidence and excluded from the `internal` labels used by `forge-review-eval`. Most checklist topics are already owned by Forge's generic rubric; the conservative scoring change is therefore limited to adding `functional_requirements.decision_rules_and_precedence`, adding `acceptance_criteria.requirement_coverage`, and refining `dependencies.dependency_readiness`, with no numeric weight, gate, or band changes. Richer contradiction, supersession, enum-domain, and scope analysis belongs in advisory deep review, where exact spans, compatible alternatives, rejected candidates, and model disagreement remain auditable. The MicroDrama PDF can anchor regression tests and content-validity hypotheses, but only blinded independent human review of representative internal PRDs can justify organization-facing claims or calibration.

## Eight source families converge on decision-ready evidence, not one template

The first-party sources do not establish a universal PRD form. They converge instead on **traceable decisions that connect an evidenced user problem to measurable outcomes, implementable behavior, owned dependencies, applicable protections, and a reversible release path**. Atlassian asks who is affected, what the problem is, why it matters, what is out of scope, and how requirements relate to users and importance; GOV.UK asks teams to understand the problem and wider journey before building, include people with access needs, define success, secure data, and operate reliably; GitLab expects validated problems, explicit testing and completion conditions, dependencies, telemetry, rollout plans, and documented decisions ([Atlassian Problem Framing](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/problem-framing); [Atlassian PRD template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/product-requirements); [GOV.UK discovery](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works); [GOV.UK Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard); [GitLab Product Development Flow](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product-development/how-we-work/product-development-flow/); [GitLab rollout plans](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/workflow/development-processes/rollout-plans/)). Working Backwards material is useful only as lower-weight practitioner evidence from former Amazon executives, not an official Amazon corporate standard; its customer/problem, alternatives, dependencies, and internal-FAQ principles are retained only where maintained first-party sources converge ([Working Backwards PR/FAQ](https://workingbackwards.com/resources/working-backwards-pr-faq/)). AWS establishes Operational Readiness Reviews as a maintained topic, but the research fetch did not expose enough body text to support granular AWS claims, so the report does not invent them ([AWS Operational Readiness Reviews](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/operational-readiness-reviews/operational-readiness-reviews.html)).

Google's public material reinforces precise interfaces, user-journey reliability objectives, graceful degradation, observability, staged launches, rollback, accessibility, and privacy review. AIP-158 supplies a concrete pagination contract, AIP-193 requires machine-readable actionable errors, and Google's SRE material ties launch readiness to capacity, dependency behavior, tests, monitoring, canaries, ownership, and recovery; these are transferable evidence expectations, not proof of a mandatory Google PRD schema ([Google AIP-158](https://google.aip.dev/158); [Google AIP-193](https://google.aip.dev/193); [Google Cloud reliability](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/define-reliability-based-on-user-experience-goals); [Google Cloud graceful degradation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/graceful-degradation); [Google SRE launch checklist](https://sre.google/sre-book/launch-checklist/); [Google SRE reliable launches](https://sre.google/sre-book/reliable-product-launches/); [web.dev accessibility](https://web.dev/learn/accessibility/); [Google Cloud privacy guidance](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/security/meet-regulatory-compliance-and-privacy-needs)). Firebase supports an objective metric, additional metrics, sufficient users and duration, and meaningful results, but it is product documentation rather than a universal experimentation policy ([Firebase A/B Testing](https://firebase.google.com/docs/ab-testing/ab-concepts)).

Microsoft and LinkedIn add the strongest public basis for measurable service behavior and trustworthy experimentation. Microsoft defines SLOs as measurable targets for customer interactions, recommends threat modeling and security testing throughout the lifecycle, and advocates progressive quality-gated deployment with health checks and recovery. LinkedIn describes deterministic experiment assignment, independence across concurrent experiments, sample-ratio-mismatch checks, combined automated and manual accessibility testing, and measured near-real-time personalization requirements ([Microsoft reliability metrics](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/reliability/metrics); [Microsoft SDL](https://www.microsoft.com/securityengineering/sdl/practices); [Microsoft safe deployments](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/operational-excellence/safe-deployments); [LinkedIn variant assignment](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/ab-testing-experimentation/a-b-testing-variant-assignment); [LinkedIn accessibility testing](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/accessibility/automated-accessibility-testing); [LinkedIn near-real-time personalization](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/member-customer-experience/near-real-time-features-for-near-real-time-personalization)). No reviewed Microsoft or LinkedIn source defines a canonical company-wide PRD score, and public responsible-AI principles do not prove that this entertainment recommender is regulated or that a particular control is legally required ([Microsoft Responsible AI](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/ai/responsible-ai)).

This evidence requires a strict epistemic partition:

| Layer | Meaning | Permitted use | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| `public_source_fact` | A cited first-party source says something in its own context. | Establish transferable content expectations and source provenance. | “Google/Microsoft/etc. would reject this PRD,” or “this is universal policy.” |
| `agent_interpretation` | An agent relates a public principle to an exact PRD passage or absence. | Generate review candidates and explain why a decision could be non-actionable. | Treat the interpretation as another source fact or human judgment. |
| `proxy_label` | Multiple synthetic reviews reconcile a candidate into `supported`, `hard_negative`, `compatible`, `contested`, or `unclear`. | Regression fixtures, diagnostic transfer tests, and proposals for human review. | Calibration, organization validity, severity prediction, or headline model accuracy. |
| `implementation_recommendation` | A conservative Forge change derived from existing ownership plus proxy evidence. | Small field-definition edits, evidence expectations, tests, and advisory candidate coverage. | Numeric weight/gate/band changes or automatic score effects from deep-review findings. |

### Preserved disagreements

**Mood persistence (`PXY-MD-005`) is a conflict only if both statements govern the same mode.** The Microsoft/LinkedIn review notes that a 30-day outage fallback could deliberately override session-only normal behavior; the label therefore remains blocker-unless-scoped rather than deterministic contradiction. **Rule-based and MAB ranking can coexist** as control/treatment, shadow evaluation, or fallback, so `PXY-MD-002` concerns missing activation and exposure states, not coexistence itself. **Disliked or hard-skipped exclusion versus tail placement is plausibly surface- or pool-scoped** because Player End has a constrained similar-content pool while the discovery feed is broader; candidate status should remain `unclear` until a product-by-state matrix aligns scopes. **A later backlog may supersede earlier ranking text**, but “latest” or later placement alone cannot mechanically establish normative supersession; the deep-review classifier should retain `supersedes`, `compatible`, and `unclear` outcomes. **The panel's P0/P1 assignments differ**, especially for experiment validity, accessibility, privacy, reliability, and rollout. Those differences are not noise to average away: they show that public-source proxies cannot calibrate severity or decide an organization's launch gate.

## Three weight-neutral edits improve evidence without duplicating coverage

Forge's current generic rubric already owns the great majority of panel concerns: `problem_statement` owns problem, affected users, and evidence; `success_metrics` owns primary metric, baseline, target, and window; `functional_requirements` owns flows, preconditions, behavior, and launch priority; `acceptance_criteria` owns observable and boundary outcomes; `edge_cases_and_states` owns degraded and accessibility coverage; `instrumentation`, `dependencies`, and `operational_readiness` own events, dependencies, service behavior, alerts, support, and recovery; `risk_compliance` owns data handling; rollout fields own entry/promotion/pause/stop behavior; and `open_questions_owned` owns unresolved decisions, owners, and dates. The core gap is not topic presence. It is whether overlapping rules resolve to one behavior and whether every launch-critical rule has a test oracle.

| Recommendation | Current criterion/field ownership | Minimal change | Multiple-source support | Why it is conservative |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Add runtime collision evidence. | `functional_requirements`; existing `prioritisation` concerns delivery priority, not runtime precedence. | Add required field `decision_rules_and_precedence`: “For behaviors with overlapping conditions, ranked inputs, or multiple applicable rules, identify the authoritative order, tie-break, default, and fallback; otherwise explicitly state that no rule collision applies.” Question: “When two rules can apply at once, which one wins, and what default or fallback applies?” | Google ADRs preserve decision context, options, status, and consequences ([Google ADR guidance](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/architecture-decision-records)); GitLab expects accurate maintained decisions ([GitLab flow](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product-development/how-we-work/product-development-flow/)); Atlassian DACI records options, decision, and action ownership without requiring DACI itself ([Atlassian DACI](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci)). | One field in an existing criterion, explicit no-collision credit for simple products, no new criterion or numeric change. It transfers to payments, permissions, playback, and workflows rather than encoding recommendation vocabulary. |
| Add requirement-to-test traceability. | `acceptance_criteria`; current `criteria` and `failure_and_boundary_criteria` can be satisfied by isolated examples. | Add required field `requirement_coverage`: “Map every launch-critical functional requirement and decision-rule boundary to at least one observable pass/fail case.” Question: “Which pass/fail case covers each must-have requirement and each boundary where behavior changes?” | Atlassian expects requirements tied to user stories and importance ([Atlassian PRD template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/product-requirements)); GitLab requires explicit testing and definition of done before progression ([GitLab flow](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product-development/how-we-work/product-development-flow/)); GOV.UK requires production-like testing and QA oversight ([GOV.UK reliable service](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-14-operate-a-reliable-service)). | It asks for a mapping rather than more examples. Full mechanical completeness will later require a ledger; the field alone must not be described as proof of complete coverage. |
| Refine material-dependency behavior. | Existing `dependencies.dependency_readiness`. | Refine description to require, for each material team/service/vendor/model/data source, owner, interface/readiness condition, freshness or availability expectation where applicable, failure impact, and stale/unavailable fallback. | Google asks teams to identify dependencies and graceful degradation ([Google graceful degradation](https://docs.cloud.google.com/architecture/framework/reliability/graceful-degradation)); Microsoft asks for critical dependencies, failure points, blast radius, degradation, and recovery ([Microsoft reliability principles](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/reliability/principles)); GOV.UK and GitLab require constraints/dependencies and credible readiness ([GOV.UK discovery](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works); [GitLab Product Processes](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product/product-processes/)). | This strengthens an existing field rather than adding a score dimension. “Where applicable” avoids false requirements for static or non-temporal dependencies. |

Several other improvements should be **anchored examples and tests, not new fields**. A solution overview must not satisfy `problem_statement.problem`; a raw uplift target must not satisfy the entire metric/decision contract; business events must not satisfy operational health observability; runtime fallbacks must not satisfy release rollback; team names must not satisfy accountable closure; bare private links must count only as discoverability unless version, status, owner, and controlling requirement are verifiable. Accessibility, privacy/security, reliability, and rollout remain applicability-sensitive: the rubric should reward a source-backed `not applicable` rationale rather than impose a prose quota. Unsupported quantitative assertions such as “~0.001% chances” or “10× more informative” are hypotheses unless sourced; an explicitly labeled measurable target can receive planning credit without being treated as historical evidence.

The panel does **not** justify numeric changes. Public and synthetic cases can improve content validity, adversarial coverage, and transfer diagnostics, but cannot set criterion weights, gates, bands, severity, expected score movement, or claims about reduced rework. A new field changes whether an existing criterion is `present`, `partial`, or `missing`; it does not repair the fixed verdict scale's coarse distance between one missing field and several missing fields. Any future gate or aggregate change requires representative internal PRDs and independent blinded human labels under Forge's validation protocol.

## Deep review should retain every candidate, including rejections

MicroDrama's most damaging defects are relational: individually explicit statements disagree in boundary, phase, enum, persistence, precedence, or authority. Field scoring can require a canonical rule and test mapping, but it cannot safely infer that two paraphrased statements conflict. The prior architecture research therefore remains the correct boundary: preserve an immutable `ClaimOccurrence` for each uniquely located source span, attach bounded `ClaimInterpretation` records for each model run, project verified claims into a small Forge-owned typed graph, generate candidates from deterministic shared keys, and keep a complete consistency ledger. The graph is an advisory projection, never a second corpus or scoring authority. Different source spans are not merged merely because an agent thinks they are equivalent, and repeated model runs are not independent source facts ([ContractNLI](https://aclanthology.org/2021.findings-emnlp.164/); [FEVER](https://aclanthology.org/N18-1074/)).

The proxy ledger adds concrete candidate types for the deep-review roadmap: `decision_rule`, `ranked_source`, `state_variable`, `enum_domain`, `schema_field`, `policy_scope`, `dependency_status`, `surface`, and `activation_state`. Python may confirm impossible ranges, disjoint normalized exact values, incompatible exact dates, duplicate ranks, broken references, and explicit supersession defects. Mood lifetime, fallback exceptions, watched-content placement, product-surface scope, implicit supersession, and source-of-truth intent require constrained closed-set classification with `compatible`, `scoped_exception`, `supersedes`, `contradiction`, `duplicate`, and `unclear`; disagreement resolves to `unclear`, not a positive finding. HANS shows why lexical overlap cannot prove contradiction, while CheckList supports capability-focused and metamorphic testing rather than one aggregate score ([HANS](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1334/); [CheckList](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.442/)).

Deep-review fixtures should include every defect label above, every hard negative, and controlled transformations: move the two claims across batch boundaries; reorder sections; duplicate a compatible example; rename recommendation terms to payments or logistics terms; add explicit product/phase/fallback scope; add an explicit supersession marker; change a number, unit, or polarity; and inject instructions into source text. Evaluation should separately measure exact-span recall, quote correctness, candidate recall, classifier precision/recall by type, `unclear` rate, three-run agreement, duplicate findings, false-positive findings per PRD, and missed blockers. “No finding” can mean only that no candidate survived the declared extraction, generator, classifier, and verification versions; it cannot mean the document is contradiction-free.

## Six steps convert proxy evidence into safe implementation work

**Priority 1: freeze the diagnostic artifact and provenance contract.** Materialize the 29 labels above with source hash, exact block/offset identity, panel-review records, public-source registry, explicit disagreements, and prohibited-use flags. Add a loader-level type barrier so `forge-review-eval` cannot ingest proxy artifacts as `internal` labels. This is the prerequisite for every later regression because a Markdown table alone is not a stable evaluation object.

**Priority 2: make the three weight-neutral rubric edits.** Add `functional_requirements.decision_rules_and_precedence`, add `acceptance_criteria.requirement_coverage`, and refine `dependencies.dependency_readiness`. Keep every criterion weight, gate assignment, and band threshold byte-for-byte unchanged. Update Forge's decision record and roadmap when implementing the product change, and describe the generic rubric as a versioned expert-prior instrument rather than calibrated.

**Priority 3: add schema, extraction, and hard-negative tests.** Require descriptions and remediation questions for new required fields; test a neutral collision-positive fixture, an overlapping-rules negative fixture, an explicit no-collision fixture, ten requirements with only two acceptance examples, a complete requirement/test matrix, dependency stale/unavailable behavior, embedded `TBD`/“to be decided” values, and positive statements explaining that a former TBD is resolved. Snapshot weights, gates, and bands. Add accessibility, risk, rollout, instrumentation, and ownership fixtures that distinguish materially present evidence from complete readiness.

**Priority 4: extend advisory deep-review candidate coverage.** Add generic subject keys for ranked rules, state lifetimes, activation states, enum/domain definitions, schema fields, dependency status, policy scope, product surface, and explicit authority/supersession. Start with `PXY-MD-003`, `004`, `005`, `006`, `007`, `008`, and `011`, while retaining `HN-006` through `HN-010` as mandatory negatives. Keep consequences and requested decisions template-rendered from verified facts; no model-authored severity, consumer, or score effect should reach users.

**Priority 5: run transfer and metamorphic diagnostics.** Evaluate the same rubric and deep-review version on MicroDrama recommendations, the earlier Micro Dramas document, Rush, and neutral cross-industry fixtures. Report quote correctness, field-extraction stability, candidate recall, false positives, duplicate findings, and invariance to section order, batch boundaries, repeated compatible examples, entity renaming, explicit exceptions, and supersession. Keep these results outside calibration and headline accuracy.

**Priority 6: replace proxy authority with blinded human evidence when available.** Use at least two independent reviewers per representative internal PRD, three where role disagreement matters, preserve individual labels before adjudication, include a candidate-free pass for missed findings, and preregister holdout, thresholds, and disagreement handling. Only that study can justify organization-specific terminology, numeric weight/gate/band changes, calibrated review ordering, or claims that the rubric predicts actionability. Until then, ship any deep-review expansion as advisory and describe proxy-derived rubric edits as evidence-backed hypotheses.

## Conclusion

The proxy panel's most useful result is not its unanimous “not ready” judgment; it is the boundary it exposes between **topic coverage** and **decision coherence**. MicroDrama contains scope, cohorts, instrumentation, safety intent, model phases, and fallbacks, yet teams could still build incompatible systems. Forge should respond by asking for one authoritative collision policy, one requirement-to-test mapping, and complete material-dependency behavior, while leaving semantic contradiction discovery in a source-bound advisory ledger.

Validation remains explicitly limited. The panel reviewed one public PDF without its private annexes, production system, legal context, telemetry, or organization policy; its agents are neither independent humans nor representatives of the cited companies; public sources vary by product and context; and no representative internal corpus or accepted error thresholds exist. The artifact can improve test coverage, reveal false positives, and guide conservative field wording. It cannot establish organization validation, human agreement, calibrated severity, legal compliance, score accuracy, optimal weights, launch readiness, or predictive value.
