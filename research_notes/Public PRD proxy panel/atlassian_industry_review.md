# Public PRD proxy panel: Atlassian and industry review

Research cutoff: 25 September 2026. External pages were checked on that date. This is a synthetic third proxy-expert review based on public material, not independent human validation, user research, legal advice, or evidence that any cited organization would score this PRD the same way.

Source weighting used here: maintained first-party guidance (GOV.UK, Atlassian, GitLab, AWS, Google) > detailed practitioner material from former Amazon executives > reviewer inference. “Require” below means “the cited source expects this in its own context”; the generic proposals are narrower cross-source inferences, not claims of a universal PRD template.

## What public guidance expects across the requested dimensions

### Takeaway

The durable cross-source standard is not a particular template. It is decision-ready traceability: an evidenced user problem leads to a bounded outcome, testable behavior, explicit dependencies and ownership, measurable success and harm limits, and a safe path to operate, release, observe, and reverse the change.

#### Standards matrix

#### Source-authority note

- Atlassian, GOV.UK, GitLab, AWS, and Google SRE are maintained first-party sources for their organizations. Their guidance is directly citable but reflects their contexts. ([Atlassian PRD template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/product-requirements); [GOV.UK Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard); [GitLab Product Development Flow](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product-development/how-we-work/product-development-flow/); [AWS ORR](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/operational-readiness-reviews/operational-readiness-reviews.html); [Google SRE canarying](https://sre.google/workbook/canarying-releases/).)
- The Working Backwards PR/FAQ page is detailed practitioner guidance from Working Backwards LLC, associated with former Amazon executives Colin Bryar and Bill Carr, not an official Amazon corporate standard. It is therefore used as lower-weight evidence and only where it converges with maintained first-party sources. ([Working Backwards PR/FAQ](https://workingbackwards.com/resources/working-backwards-pr-faq/), ©2021–2026 on page.)

### Inferences

- “Complete PRD” should mean sufficiently unambiguous for affected disciplines to make the same implementation and release decisions, not compliance with one company’s document headings.
- The most generalizable fields are: user/problem/evidence; intended outcome; in/out scope; primary and exceptional journeys; testable requirements; dependencies/constraints; metrics and guardrails; privacy/accessibility/security; operational and rollout/rollback readiness; owners/decisions/open questions.
- Evidence quality should be scored separately from prose presence. A polished assertion without research, baseline, calculation, or linked evidence should not receive the same credit as a traceable claim.

### Gaps

- Atlassian’s pages expose copyright years rather than per-page “last updated” dates; they were live when checked on 25 September 2026.
- GitLab’s generated handbook pages did not expose a simple page-update date in fetched text; they were live and maintained when checked.
- AWS ORR pages returned their title but not substantive body text to the fetcher. Detailed AWS-specific ORR claims are intentionally omitted rather than reconstructed from memory.
- No public source establishes one universal PRD schema or says every requirement must physically live in a single PRD. Linked evidence can satisfy the intent if it is accessible, current, and decision-ready.

## Exact target-document defects and ambiguities

### Takeaway

The PRD contains substantial ranking and event detail, useful fallbacks, one explicit out-of-scope item, and three owned Phase 2 open points. It is nevertheless not consistently actionable: it lacks an evidenced user problem and outcome model, leaves key values unresolved, contradicts itself on skip semantics and phase timing, and does not define privacy/accessibility readiness or a production rollout/rollback contract.

### Cited Findings

#### Document strengths, using its exact labels

- **“1. Overview”** accurately identifies the system boundary at a high level: “This document consolidates all product, data, and ML requirements for the MicroDrama recommendation system on XstreamPlay app” (PRD p.1). This is a solution/system overview, not a user-problem statement.
- **“2.1 User type definitions”** defines “Existing User” as someone who “Has streamed ≥1 content on Xstream play app” and “Cold Start User” as “All the remaining users” (PRD p.4). These are implementable signal-availability cohorts.
- **“3.1 User Journeys to use the same product flow”** describes two entry paths and a next-content sequence (PRD p.12), and **“6.3 Model and Final Merging Simulations”** covers ML-down, persona-down, exhausted-catalog, and changed-signal scenarios (PRD pp.15–17). This is meaningful edge/fallback coverage, though not a full journey or operating plan.
- **“[Out of MVP Scope]Product 5 - New in your language(out of scope in MVP)”** is an explicit non-goal (PRD p.13).
- **“Open Points : Phase 2”** includes three items with role owners and priorities, for example “Catalog depth matrix…” owned by “Catalog” at “P1” and TTMK fallback owned by “Product + BE” at “P1” (PRD p.19). This is useful but too narrow to resolve the document’s launch-critical ambiguities.

#### Page-backed contradictions and unresolved semantics

1. **Skip ranges are contradictory and sometimes mathematically impossible.** Page 7 says “Soft Skipped Contents(21secs>=X>=40secs)” and “Hard Skipped Contents(>=5 secs)” (PRD p.7). Page 9 instead defines hard as “watch duration ≤ 5secs,” medium as `6 <= duration < 20`, soft as `20 <= duration < 50`, and no-skip as `>=50` (PRD p.9). Page 18 repeats “Soft Skipped Contents(21secs>=X>=40secs)” while defining hard as “<=5 secs” and no-skip as “> 40 secs” (PRD p.18). A client, DP pipeline, BE service, and QA suite cannot all implement one taxonomy from this.
2. **The ranking order changes across authoritative-looking sections.** Page 7 places “Disliked Contents” before “Watched sampling/ No_skip” and episodic watched content; page 8’s “Final Collection Ordering” places watched content immediately after disliked; page 18’s backlog puts “Disliked Contents” after watched `>20% but <=90%` and watched sampling (PRD pp.7–8, 18). No statement identifies which text supersedes which, despite page 18 saying the backlog is “to be prioritised.”
3. **Mood-picker rules conflict.** “All Users” says first picker after `N=3`, next after `N=10`, and maximum twice per lifecycle (PRD p.10). The appendix instead describes “The 3-skip nudge,” “the 5-skip re-picker,” and “genre-specific rejection at 5 consecutive same-genre skips” (PRD pp.19–20). These produce different UI behavior and analytics.
4. **Signal duration boundaries conflict.** Page 7 uses liked/watched around 20%, hard `>=5 secs`, and sampling no-skip `>40%`; page 9 uses seconds and a 50-second no-skip boundary; page 18 uses a 40-second no-skip boundary (PRD pp.7, 9, 18). The document alternates seconds and percentages without a normalization rule.
5. **Launch and model timing conflict.** Page 1 labels ML recommended feed “MVP Phase 2” at “4-5 weeks from launch” and trending at “2–4 weeks post-launch” (PRD p.1). Page 3 says manual curation remains the source of truth until ML/analytics are ready in “30-60 days post MVP launch” (PRD p.3). Page 4 says MAB uses interactions “from the D0 for all users” (PRD p.4). The phase table says MAB is “Launch day,” CBF is “1-2 weeks post-launch,” another CBF is “3-4 weeks,” and collaborative filtering is “8–12 weeks” (PRD pp.14–15). Product 4 says trending goes live “2-3 weeks after MVP launch” (PRD p.13). Release planning, data readiness, and experiment assignment therefore have no single timeline.
6. **Source-composition decisions remain open.** “Variant 1” uses strict source priority and “No round robin”; “Variant 2” uses configurable ratios and round robin (PRD p.4). The document does not select a launch variant, name its approver, or supply decision criteria. Later “Final Collection Ordering” reads as a third ordering (PRD p.8).
7. **Language fallback is known but unresolved for a named audience.** The PRD says Hindi is primary and English secondary (PRD p.1), while open point 10 asks to “Define fallback experience” for Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, and Kannada users (PRD p.19). It is marked Phase 2/P1 even though user-selected onboarding language participates in launch ranking (PRD pp.5–8). The launch behavior for those users is not determinate.
8. **A content-safety prerequisite has no release gate.** The PRD says, “Without this, erotic content cannot be excluded” and “Without this, A-rated content cannot be filtered from recommendations” (PRD p.8). It does not state completion evidence, validation coverage, owner sign-off, fail-closed behavior when flags are null/stale, or whether launch is blocked. Because this affects age-sensitive content, “must be done” without a gate is inadequate.
9. **Data schema details are internally suspect.** `genre_tags` is typed as `string` but described as “All genre tags attached” (PRD p.12), while earlier examples use array syntax such as `genres : [“Romance", "Comedy"]` (PRD p.2). Event producers and consumers could encode incompatible payloads.
10. **Unsupported certainty appears in operational claims.** “When ML services is down(~0.001% chances)” provides no source, SLO, measurement window, or owner (PRD p.16). The appendix asserts “A hard skip (0–3 sec) is 10× more informative” and “contextually coherent recos get clicked more than random mixes” without linked evidence (PRD pp.19–20). These should be labeled hypotheses or supported with data.

#### Metrics, guardrails, operations, and ownership defects

- The only concrete experiment promotion rule is “Promote MAB if CTR +5% vs control” (PRD p.15). The PRD does not define CTR numerator/denominator, eligible impression, attribution window, population, baseline period, sample-size/power or uncertainty rule, experiment duration, novelty effects, or decision owner. A `+5%` relative versus percentage-point interpretation alone can reverse the decision.
- No guardrails are defined for watch quality/completion, retention, skips, picker interruption, age-rating leakage, erotic-content leakage, language mismatch, diversity, bias, latency, error rate, empty results, repeated content, catalog-provider balance, complaints, or privacy/accessibility outcomes. GOV.UK explicitly updated reliability guidance in January 2026 to include monitoring user outcomes and ethical issues such as bias, not only technical faults. ([GOV.UK point 14](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard/point-14-operate-a-reliable-service).)
- “When ML services is down” and “PersonaIQ / Watch History down” have feed fallbacks (PRD pp.16–17), but there are no SLOs, alert thresholds, dashboards, on-call owner, incident severity, capacity target, recovery objective, or runbook. “Increase Aerospike connections” appears only as backlog item 7 (PRD p.18), without the capacity evidence or completion criterion needed for launch.
- Dependencies are numerous and material: content tagging, parent-show mapping, BE flags, CW API fields, DP persona pipeline, catalog curation, clients, Zion config, ML output, and partner catalog feeds (PRD pp.1–11, 20–21). Most are phrased as team obligations, not tracked commitments with named accountable owner, status, due date, interface/version, readiness proof, and fail behavior.
- Named ownership is inconsistent. Page 1 says `content_type` is “To be decided by Supply” and names Rohit Kumar (PRD p.1); page 19 has role owners for three Phase 2 open points. The launch variant, thresholds, metrics, safety gate, privacy/security acceptance, release approval, experiment decision, and rollback decision have no named owner/approver.

### Inferences

- **Proxy verdict:** not ready for consistent cross-functional implementation or controlled launch without clarification. The highest-risk blockers are the contradictory skip/ranking contract, unresolved launch source/timeline, missing age/content-safety gate, undefined experiment decision rule and guardrails, and absent privacy/rollback readiness.
- The PRD’s most useful material can be retained. The feed examples, signal taxonomy, failure fallbacks, and open-point table should become implementation appendices beneath a shorter authoritative contract rather than being discarded.
- A single source-of-truth precedence rule is essential: mark requirements as `decided`, `hypothesis`, `open`, `superseded`, or `future`, each with version/date and owner. Without this, adding more prose increases ambiguity.

### Gaps

- Linked Figma, Google Drive, SharePoint, API, ML, HLD, DP, and solution documents were not available as part of the assigned PDF review. They may contain missing evidence, interfaces, or controls, but inaccessible links cannot cure ambiguity in the PDF for a reader who must act from it.
- The PDF does not identify its author, approver, approval status, version history, or intended launch date. Its title suffix suggests an export date, and page 18 says technical solutioning is latest as of 29 June 2026, but neither establishes document governance.
- The review does not determine whether Indian privacy, child-safety, media, or accessibility law applies. It identifies missing product requirements and decision evidence, not legal conclusions.

## Generic rubric changes and exclusions

### Takeaway

The rubric should reward evidence-backed, internally consistent decision contracts and apply risk-sensitive gates. It should not mandate PR/FAQ prose, DACI labels, GOV.UK publishing duties, GitLab workflow labels, AWS review ceremony, or any other organization’s implementation mechanics.

#### Scoring mechanics implied by the evidence

- Separate **presence**, **specificity**, **evidence**, **consistency**, and **ownership/readiness**. A criterion can be present but non-actionable.
- Use **applicability triggers**. For example, behavioral profiling triggers privacy review; autoplay and interactive UI trigger accessibility; live recommendation changes trigger rollout/rollback; third-party feeds trigger dependency readiness.
- Use **hard gates sparingly** for conditions that can cause inconsistent implementation or unsafe launch: contradictory normative requirements, unknown launch scope, missing safety/privacy controls for applicable high-risk data/content, unresolved critical dependencies, and no recovery path for a material production change.
- Give explicit credit for **negative decisions**: non-goals, rejected alternatives, “not applicable” with rationale, and stop/no-go criteria. Atlassian, GOV.UK, and Working Backwards all treat boundaries and no-go reasoning as useful decision evidence. ([Atlassian PRD template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/product-requirements); [GOV.UK discovery](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/agile-delivery/how-the-discovery-phase-works); [Working Backwards](https://workingbackwards.com/resources/working-backwards-pr-faq/).)

#### What should not be added

1. **Do not require a PR/FAQ, press-release fiction, quotes, TAM, willingness-to-pay, or “better/faster/cheaper” language for every PRD.** Those are useful Working Backwards mechanisms for new bets, but can be irrelevant to internal, regulatory, reliability, platform, or corrective work. Preserve the underlying customer/problem/alternative/risk tests instead. ([Working Backwards PR/FAQ](https://workingbackwards.com/resources/working-backwards-pr-faq/).)
2. **Do not require DACI labels, one specific RACI variant, silent-reading meetings, Jira labels, GitLab milestones, feature-flag percentages, or AWS ORR meetings.** Require clear decision authority, accountable ownership, controlled exposure, and readiness evidence; let teams choose process. ([Atlassian DACI](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci); [GitLab flow](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product-development/how-we-work/product-development-flow/); [GitLab rollout plans](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/workflow/development-processes/rollout-plans/).)
3. **Do not require GOV.UK-specific public-performance publication, offline assisted-digital channels, open-source duties, or government approval controls for private products.** Retain generic accessibility, privacy, whole-journey, measurement, and reliability principles. ([GOV.UK Service Standard](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/service-standard).)
4. **Do not add duplicate criteria for objective, KPI, success metric, experiment metric, telemetry, and acceptance metric without defining their relationships.** One traceable outcome model can serve all of them; separate guardrails and operational SLIs only where they answer different decisions.
5. **Do not reward document length, number of headings, diagrams, links, or implementation detail by itself.** The target PRD demonstrates why: 21 pages and extensive tables coexist with unresolved launch semantics. Atlassian describes supporting artifacts as context, while GitLab requires the underlying issue description to remain accurate. ([Atlassian PRD template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/product-requirements); [GitLab flow](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product-development/how-we-work/product-development-flow/).)
6. **Do not force every edge case into the main narrative.** Require coverage and traceability, but allow linked test plans, API schemas, threat models, accessibility assessments, runbooks, and experiment plans when accessible and versioned. Atlassian explicitly supports consolidated linked documentation; GitLab’s flow repeatedly permits linked readiness material. ([Atlassian PRD template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/product-requirements); [GitLab flow](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product-development/how-we-work/product-development-flow/).)
7. **Do not convert source-specific numerical examples into universal thresholds.** Atlassian’s 15% satisfaction example, Google’s illustrative 5% canary, and the target PRD’s +5% CTR rule are not generic defaults. The rubric should test whether a threshold is defined, justified, measurable, and tied to a decision. ([Atlassian PRD template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/product-requirements); [Google SRE canarying](https://sre.google/workbook/canarying-releases/).)

### Inferences

- The best rubric revision is likely a small set of cross-cutting quality dimensions plus conditional risk modules, not a longer checklist copied from all sources.
- A contradiction detector deserves first-class status. In this PRD, a naive presence checklist would award points for user definitions, journeys, metrics, failure states, and owners while missing that teams would still build different systems.
- Rubric wording should explicitly accept authoritative linked artifacts but require accessibility, version/date, owner, and a concise in-PRD statement of the decision they govern.
- “Synthetic proxy evidence” should remain visible in any downstream calibration record. This review can suggest content validity and reveal defects, but it cannot establish inter-rater reliability, predictive validity, or calibrated score thresholds.

### Gaps

- Public guidance establishes broad content expectations but does not provide empirical weights for a generic PRD scoring model. Weighting and gate thresholds require labeled real PRDs and independent human review.
- The sources do not resolve how much detail belongs in a PRD versus linked delivery artifacts. That boundary should be calibrated to the rubric’s use case and the organization’s artifact ecosystem.
- No source supports treating the target PRD’s domain-specific ML choices, skip thresholds, catalog rules, or model sequence as generic rubric requirements. They should be judged for clarity, evidence, safety, and testability, not correctness from these public sources alone.
