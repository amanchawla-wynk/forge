# Unified Checkout Experience PRD

## 1. Problem

Customers find the current checkout experience frustrating and inconsistent.
Our customers are the people affected by this.
Feedback from several stakeholders suggests this is a real and pressing problem.
If we do not act, the situation is likely to get worse over time.

## 2. Success metrics

The primary metric is checkout conversion.
Conversion is currently lower than we would like.
We want to improve it meaningfully after launch.
We will review performance regularly once the feature is live.
We will also make sure that other key metrics do not get worse.

## 3. Non-goals

Anything not described in this document is considered out of scope.
We are not trying to solve every checkout problem at once.

## 4. Requirements

The checkout should work reliably across all supported platforms.
The experience must be intuitive and easy for customers to understand.
The system should handle errors gracefully.
All of the above are important for a successful launch.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

The feature is complete when checkout works as described above.
Quality assurance will confirm that the experience behaves as expected.

## 6. Error and empty states

Errors should be handled in a user-friendly way with helpful messaging.
Empty states should guide the customer toward the next logical action.

## 7. Instrumentation

We will add appropriate analytics events to the checkout flow.
These events will let us understand how the feature is performing.

## 8. Dependencies

This work depends on the usual platform and backend teams.
There may be other dependencies that we discover during implementation.

## 9. Privacy and compliance

This feature follows all standard company policies for customer data.
The relevant teams will be consulted as needed.

## 10. Rollout

We will roll this out in a phased and careful manner.
If anything goes wrong we will take appropriate corrective action.

## 11. Open questions

A few details are still being worked through with the wider team.
These will be resolved as part of the implementation process.

## 12. Alternatives considered

We considered several other approaches to this problem.
They were not chosen because the proposed approach is a better fit.
